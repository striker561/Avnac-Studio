# Rule: Binding and Book Construction

**Domain:** print · **ID:** `88-binding-and-book-construction`

## Purpose
Choose how the pages are held before drawing them, because the binding decides the gutter, whether
a spread can carry one image, how far the inner pages creep, and how wide the spine is. Binding is
a layout constraint and a cost line, not a finishing option chosen at the end.

## Definition
Binding is the method — saddle stitch, perfect bound, sewn, wire-o, lay-flat — plus its case:
cover boards, spine (square or rounded), endpapers, and headband. Each method opens to a different
angle, eats a different amount of the inner margin, and imposes a different page count.

## Core Principles
- Binding is a layout constraint. It fixes the usable gutter, the legibility of a spread, and
  whether a face can cross the fold; decide it before the grid.
- Saddle stitch runs in multiples of four, opens flat at the centre, and creeps toward the middle.
  It is the economical choice for thin programmes and zines; most printers prefer it under about
  64 pages, and thinner stock buys more.
- Perfect bound has a square spine, does not open flat, and takes 3–5 mm of the inner margin. A
  cross-gutter image breaks across the fold; keep faces and key content clear of it.
- Sewn, lay-flat, Swiss, and Vignelli's "sandwich" binding open flat, so one image can run across
  the spread. Cost and visible sewing are the trade.
- The spine is computed, not guessed: pages ÷ 2 × paper caliper, plus a cover allowance. Design the
  cover after the number exists, not before.
- Cover material and lamination are a pair. Laminated paper wants calendered board so the surface
  stays smooth; cloth or leather can sit on regular chipboard (p. 88).
- Endpapers and headbands are design surfaces, and the cheapest place in the object to add color.

## Techniques
1. **Choose binding from use** — flat reference (cookbook, art book, manual) wants lay-flat, sewn,
   or wire-o; a thin programme wants saddle stitch; a report or novel wants perfect bound.
2. **Compute the spine** — 128 pages at 0.12 mm caliper is 64 × 0.12 = 7.7 mm, plus 2 × 1 mm of
   cover, so about 9.7 mm. Put that as a guide in the cover file and in the printer's spec.
3. **Set the gutter before the grid** — 15–20 mm inner margin for perfect bound, 10–12 mm for
   saddle stitch, 8–10 mm for lay-flat. Then build the grid inside what remains.
4. **Plan for creep** — in saddle stitch the innermost sheet shifts by caliper × sheets: 0.1 mm
   stock with 16 sheets moves about 1.6 mm. Either keep content inside the safe area or ask for
   creep compensation at imposition.
5. **Apply the cross-spread rule** — full-bleed images across the fold only on lay-flat, sewn, or
   saddle-stitched centres; on perfect binding keep faces 10 mm clear of the gutter.
6. **Make a dummy** — blank pages on the real stock with the real binding, before approving a
   cross-spread image or a tight inner margin.

## When to Use
- Any multi-page printed object: books, brochures, zines, catalogues, reports, programmes.
- Whenever a spread is designed as one image, or a cover carries a spine title.
- When the page count is still open: the binding may set a minimum or force a multiple of four.

## When to Avoid
- Single sheets, folded leaflets, and posters — folding and finishing rules apply there, not
  binding (see `84-print-finishing-techniques`).
- Packaging: a carton is a die-cut structure, not a bound book (see `86-packaging-and-dielines`).
- Under about eight pages, the object is a folded sheet; do not specify a binding for it.

## Common Mistakes
| Mistake | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Designing spreads before choosing the binding | The fold lands in a face and the layout is rebuilt | Binding decision in the first brief |
| Assuming perfect bound opens flat | The subject of a spread image disappears into the gutter | Single-page image, or resew |
| A guessed spine width | Cover art drifts off-centre and the text block sits wrong | Caliper × pages ÷ 2 plus cover allowance |
| Ignoring creep on saddle stitch | Inner pages lose their outer margin | Safe-area discipline or creep compensation |
| Laminating uncoated board | Curl and bubbles as the sheet absorbs | Calendered board for laminated covers |

## Practical Examples
A 96-page A5 catalogue on 150 g/m² text, perfect bound. The stock calipers at about 0.15 mm, so
the spine is 48 × 0.15 + 2 mm of cover, roughly 9.2 mm; the inner margin is set to 18 mm and the
outer to 12 mm, and the grid is built inside the resulting 118 mm type area.

The design calls for a two-page hero photograph. Under perfect binding the subject would sit in
the fold, so it is rebuilt as a single page plus bleed with the caption on the facing page. If the
client insists on the spread image, the job is resewn to open flat, and the extra binding cost is
quoted against the extra page-count and production time rather than absorbed silently.
