# Rule: Visual Identity Systems

**Domain:** branding · **ID:** `52-visual-identity-systems`

## Purpose
Treats logo, type, color, imagery, graphic device and motion as one system with fixed and variable parts, so five touchpoints look like one brand instead of five submissions from one designer.

## Definition
A visual identity system is the fixed set of marks and rules — logo and lockups, type, color, imagery treatment, a graphic device, motion behavior, layout skeleton — plus the rules that let it flex. Recognition comes from the invariant parts being identical everywhere, not from repeating one asset until it is boring.

## Core Principles
- Six parts make the system: mark, type, color, imagery, graphic device, motion. Anchoring two and improvising four produces drift, not flexibility.
- Separate invariants from variables before building. Invariant: mark proportions, primary color, caps treatment, image grade. Variable: crop, secondary color, density, format.
- Identity architecture comes first: branded house (one name), house of brands (independent marks), or endorsed (parent plus sub-brand). Pick one, then derive the variation rules.
- Color field: one primary, two secondary, four neutrals. The primary should be identifiable when it covers only 5% of a layout.
- Type: one primary family, one secondary. A third family is a system failure, not an enrichment.
- One image treatment applied to 100% of photographs does more for recognition than any new logo, because photography occupies the most area.

## Techniques
1. **Asset ladder** — five deliverables: mark, lockup, color field, type pair, image rule. Test each at 400×400 thumbnail and at 3 m poster scale before adding anything.
2. **Tokens in Avnac** — name fills and strokes (`brand/primary`, `brand/ink-70`, `brand/rule`) and reuse the swatch; a `group` per lockup so scaling preserves ratios.
3. **Responsive mark set** — full lockup, stacked, icon plus wordmark, icon only, each with a minimum width so nobody has to guess.
4. **Derive the graphic device from the mark** — take one of its angles, radii or proportions (a 15° cut, a 4:3 crop, an 8 px rule) and repeat it. Devices carry recognition where the logo cannot fit.
5. **Motion rule** — 200–400 ms, one easing curve, logo reveals under 600 ms. In static work, use a frozen frame of the motion as a layout cue.

## When to Use
- Any brand with more than one touchpoint: site, packaging, social grid, invoice, signage.
- When a client needs templates more than they need a new logo.
- When work made by three different hands starts looking like three brands.

## When to Avoid
- Single-asset jobs: build only the pieces the job uses, or you will ship unused rules.
- Brands whose architecture is undecided; a system built on an unresolved structure locks in the wrong answer.

## Common Mistakes
| Mistake | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Three typefaces across touchpoints | Reading rhythm differs; nothing coheres | One family plus one secondary, maximum |
| Color used as decoration | No identifier survives without the logo | Reserve the primary for structural moments |
| A different photo grade per channel | The largest visual area stops being consistent | One grade, applied to every image |
| Sub-brands with unrelated marks | Readers cannot connect them | Shared skeleton, variable color and name |

## Practical Examples
A three-touchpoint system: primary `#0F3D3E` on `#F4EFE6`, secondary `#C86B3C`, neutrals `#111111`/`#4B4B4B`/`#E5E5E5`. Type: Inter 700 for headings, 400 for body, no third face. Device: an 8 px rule set at 45°, used as the divider under every heading. Image rule: desaturated with a warm highlight, 4:5 crops, subject at the left third. Motion: 240 ms ease-out. Across a 1080×1080 post, a 1200×628 card and an A5 flyer, the constants are the rule, the grade and the ink color — the logo appears in only two of the three.
