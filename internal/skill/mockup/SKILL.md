# Mockups and Presentation

## Applies when
The design exists and needs to be shown in a believable context. A mockup is a communication
tool, not a design step: fix the design first ([`113-design-quality-checklist`](../rules/113-design-quality-checklist.md)),
then present it. Mockups hide flaws and create false confidence — including yours.

## Workflow
1. **Decide what the mockup must prove**: scale, material, context of use, or craft. One mockup
   per claim.
2. **Choose the least deceptive context.** A clean device frame is honest; a heavily styled scene
   with a blurred screen is decorative. If the client will judge the design, show the design.
3. **Match perspective and light.** The artwork's vanishing point, light direction and shadow must
   agree with the scene. A screen pasted at the wrong angle reads as fake instantly.
4. **Fit, don't squash.** Preserve aspect ratio; crop with intent; respect safe areas and device
   bezels. Screens have notches, homes and bezels — place content inside the safe area.
5. **Keep effects proportional.** Add reflection, grain and shadow only at the intensity the scene
   implies. Glossy screens in a matte room destroy credibility.
6. **Show real content.** Lorem ipsum and placeholder logos undercut every presentation. Use the
   actual copy or clearly labelled representative content.
7. **Pair with the flat artwork.** Deliver the design and the mockup; the flat file is what gets
   evaluated, the mockup is what gets understood.

## Decision points
- **Device frame or environmental scene?** Frames for UI and app work; environments for
  packaging, signage, print. Environmental scenes need more craft to be believable.
- **One hero or a set?** One hero for the pitch, a small set for range (responsive, packaging
  variants). More than six mockups hides the work.
- **Photo-real or illustrated?** Photo-real for client approval; illustrated/flat for internal
  concepting and design-system docs.
- **How much scene?** Enough context to understand scale and use; not enough to distract from craft.

## Production checks
- Artwork is a real render, not a squashed screenshot.
- Screen content sits inside bezels and safe areas; no notch overlap.
- Perspective, light and shadow direction consistent.
- Aspect ratios correct for each device/format.
- Resolution sufficient for the presentation medium (2000 px+ wide for screens, 300 PPI for print).
- Deliverable includes the flat artwork and the source of the mockup scene.

## Output format
`Claim:` what the mockup proves. `Context:` scene/device, why chosen. `Files:` mockup + flat
artwork. `Honesty note:` anything the mockup hides (gloss, scale, colour shift).
